## STEP 1: DEEP RESEARCH — ACTIONABLE INSIGHTS

### Most Successful Moments (Evidence-Based)
- **Puma Suede (1968):** The shoe Kareem Abdul-Jabbar wore when he raised his fist at the 1968 Olympics. Puma has always been adjacent to countercultural moments — not leading them, but **present at the exact right time**.
- **Clyde (1973):** Named for Walt "Clyde" Frazier — one of the first signature athlete shoes. Pre-dated the Jordan concept by 12 years. Puma invented the athlete-shoe narrative and let Nike steal the spotlight.
- **FENTY PUMA by Rihanna (2015–2018):** The "Creeper" sold out in 3 hours. Rihanna transformed Puma from a #3 brand into a high-fashion conversation. Key: Puma gave Rihanna complete creative freedom — they got out of their own way.
- **A$AP Rocky x Puma Collaboration (2019):** Brought menswear luxury aesthetic to the brand.
- **Mihara Yasuhiro Collaboration (2019–present):** Deconstructed the RS-X into high fashion — weird, avant-garde, collector-level.
- **RS-X Reinvention (2018):** Chunky dad shoe at the right cultural moment — Puma read the trend early.

### What Made It Work
- **Emotional Trigger:** "I knew before everyone else." Puma's audience is contrarian cool — they choose Puma precisely because it's NOT the obvious choice.
- **Cultural Positioning:** The cool alternative. Never loudest, never first — but always more interesting to the people who matter.
- **Audience Psychology:** Puma buyers are taste-makers, not trend-followers. They reject Nike's corporate dominance narrative. They choose Puma as a statement of independent thinking.
- **Key Insight:** Puma's biggest wins come when it **partners with genuinely avant-garde creatives and lets them lead**. Its worst moments come when it tries to compete directly with Nike/Adidas on their terms.

### Visual Identity
- Leaping puma — speed, agility, unpredictability
- Bold saturated palettes: Rihanna era (blush, burgundy, fashion-week pastels); RS-X era (maximum color clash)
- Deconstructed aesthetics — Mihara's "broken" shoes intentionally imperfect
- Fashion photography language: editorial, not sport-performance
- Negative space as luxury marker

### Core Narrative Archetype
**The Underground Trendsetter** — Puma is always one step ahead, invisible to the mainstream until the mainstream catches up. Then Puma moves again.

## STEP 2: EXPERIENCE STRATEGY

### Concept: "The Invisible Influence"

The website reveals how cultural moments that the user thought were "cool" were actually quietly shaped by Puma — without anyone noticing. The experience is a **detective narrative**: the user peels back layers of cultural history to find Puma's fingerprints everywhere.

Why this works:
- Flips the "Puma is #3" perception into "Puma is the secret ingredient."
- Creates discovery-driven engagement — users feel smart for finding these connections.
- Reinforces contrarian brand identity: "The brand that doesn't need to announce itself."

### SCENE 1 — "THE SHADOW IN THE FRAME"
**Scroll Range:** 0–12%

**Environment:**
Archival black-and-white photography aesthetic. Like being inside a negative. High contrast. The space has no clear geometry — it's a photographic darkroom writ large. Red safelight illumination from below. The walls are developing photographs, slowly resolving.

**Camera Choreography:**
- Start: Camera very close to a developing photograph (macro lens feel — 100mm)
- Slowly pulls back as scroll progresses
- Reveals more of the darkroom space — never fully lit
- Lens flare from safelight on lens edge (organic, not digital)

**Object/Character Animation:**
- Developing photographs on all walls slowly reveal iconic cultural moments (track suits on musicians, Suede shoes at political events)
- A Puma Suede materializes last — it's the final element in every photograph
- The leaping cat logo appears in negative (as if stamped) — last to be seen

**Transition:**
Safelight brightens to white flash → scene 2 emerges from the overexposure

**Visual Effects:**
- Custom developing-photo shader: sepia noise resolving to clarity over time
- Chromatic aberration (heavy at edges, clearing center)
- Red point lights casting multiple shadows
- Film halation bloom around highlights

**Interaction Details:**
- Slow scroll: photos develop slowly — user can examine cultural details
- Fast scroll: flash of white, skip to next
- Hover on any photo: freeze and zoom in — discover Puma detail

**Sound Design:**
- Darkroom quiet: hum of ventilation
- Chemical drip sounds
- Deep sub-bass pulse (heartbeat of something hidden)

### SCENE 2 — "1968: THE RAISED FIST, THE QUIET STRIPE"
**Scroll Range:** 12–26%

**Environment:**
Olympic stadium, Mexico City, 1968 — but abstracted. Not photorealistic. The space is dramatic scale: stadium bowl rendered in geometry only (wireframe + selective fill). A single athletic track. A single podium rising from below. **Everything is in grey except one element: the leaping Puma on the shoe.**

**Camera Choreography:**
- Starts at crowd level, looking across to the podium
- Dramatic zoom: 85mm → 400mm telephoto compression
- Simulates the feeling of watching from far away, then being pulled into the moment
- Final frame: extreme close-up on the shoe sole during the historic raised-fist moment

**Object/Character Animation:**
- Athlete figure (wireframe, not realistic) steps onto podium in slow motion
- Fist rises — hold on fist at 2x slow-mo for 3 scroll-seconds
- Camera slowly tilts down from fist to shoe
- Puma logo glows — the only full-color, full-material element in the scene

**Transition:**
Stadium wireframe dissolves into color fragments → fragments reform as fashion runway → Scene 3

**Visual Effects:**
- Wireframe world shader — cel-shaded with selective solid surfaces
- Depth-of-field emphasizing the shoe in extreme close-up
- Atmospheric perspective (distance haze)
- Single color accent: Puma Suede royal blue

**Interaction Details:**
- Pause at 20%: athlete figure freezes, raised fist becomes a still frame — respect the moment
- Hover on logo: historical text appears: "They didn't see us coming. They never do."

**Sound Design:**
- Stadium white noise dissolves
- Silence at podium moment
- Low orchestral swell — then cuts to nothing

### SCENE 3 — "RIHANNA TOOK OVER: THE FENTY CHAPTER"
**Scroll Range:** 26–44%

**Environment:**
High-fashion void. White marble floor stretching to infinity — but the marble has Puma logo etched into its veining. Runway lights overhead: warm tungsten. The space breathes fashion week energy. The palette shifts: dusty rose, burgundy, ivory. **This is the season Puma became a fashion house.**

**Camera Choreography:**
- Runway shot: long focal length (135mm), camera positioned at runway end
- Models (abstract particle figures) walk toward camera in slow motion
- Camera slowly rises to 2.5m, slight tilt-down at end
- Final hold: low angle (0.3m) looking up at Creeper shoe

**Object/Character Animation:**
- Puma Creeper platform shoe descends from above on an invisible thread — slow, deliberate
- Shoe rotation: deliberate 4-second full turn, showcasing platform sole
- Around it: particle-fabric simulation — translucent fashion fabric billowing in zero gravity
- Creeper lands with impact shockwave that sends marble cracking in gold lines (kintsugi pattern)

**Transition:**
Marble cracks fill with gold light → gold becomes warm desert sand → Scene 4

**Visual Effects:**
- Subsurface marble material (light bleeds through thin edges)
- Gold kintsugi crack shader (procedural fracture + emission fill)
- Fabric simulation: Verlet integration cloth physics
- Particle fashion models: 80k points per figure, physics-based walk cycle

**Interaction Details:**
- Hover Creeper: fabric swirls around it magnetically
- Scroll velocity response: fast scroll = fabric tears and reforms with each scene change
- At 35%: brief dramatic pause — shoe hangs, fabric billows — 1.5s complete scroll freeze

**Sound Design:**
- Fashion week ambient: muffled crowd applause
- Textile sounds: silk, creaking leather
- Rihanna's vocal sample (one note from "Needed Me" — just the hook fragment)

### SCENE 4 — "MIHARA'S DECONSTRUCTION: THE ART OF BREAKING"
**Scroll Range:** 44–60%

**Environment:**
A sculptor's studio at night. Concrete floors with tool marks. Work lights on gooseneck stands. Scattered sketches on translucent paper (visible via backlight). The RS-X shoe exists in multiple states simultaneously — whole, broken apart, and re-assembled in the wrong order. **This is Puma when it lets artists destroy it.**

**Camera Choreography:**
- Handheld camera feel: slight organic sway (sine wave camera offset, low frequency)
- Slow circling orbit at 1.5m distance around the deconstructed shoe
- The camera bumps slightly — as if a person is walking around examining it
- Lens: 50mm — neutral, observational, artist's eye

**Object/Character Animation:**
- RS-X shoe in three states:
  1. Left: Fully assembled (original product)
  2. Center: Exploded view — components floating at varying distances
  3. Right: Mihara's version — reassembled with wrong pieces, intentionally broken beauty
- Components of center version slowly orbit the center axis
- Scroll drives: components move from state 1 → 2 → 3

**Transition:**
Deconstructed components scatter outward → become star field → Scene 5

**Visual Effects:**
- Exploded-view shader: components maintain connection lines (technical drawing aesthetic)
- Grease pencil / blueprint overlay on component labels
- Ambient occlusion heavy — studio depth
- Component orbit: orbital mechanics simulation (elliptical, varying speeds)

**Interaction Details:**
- Click any component: it enlarges, shows its name and designer modification
- Drag to rotate: exploded view responds to mouse orbit
- Scroll pace controls assembly/disassembly speed

**Sound Design:**
- Metal on concrete: tools, workshop
- Japanese ambient: gentle flute note
- Mechanical clicking as components move

**Camera Choreography:**
- Overhead birds-eye shot (top-down, 90°) slowly rotating
- Simulates command center / god's-eye view

- End position: dutch tilt (15°) — confident, slightly provocative

**Object/Character Animation:**
- RS-X shoe center frame, color-shifted: maximum saturation version
- Around it: holographic trend graphs showing competitor data (abstractly visualized)
- Data streams flow into the shoe from all directions — converging on it as the origin point
- Shoe's chunky silhouette morphs subtly (thickness increases) as "cultural weight" is shown

**Transition:**
Neon grid powers down → darkness → one spotlight → Scene 6

**Visual Effects:**
- Neon grid: emissive material, HDR bloom
- Holographic data: custom shader (scan lines + transparency gradient)
- Data stream particles: trail effects, speed varies with data "intensity"
- Dutch tilt held static — screen-space rotation

**Interaction Details:**
- Tilt head: camera responds (deviceOrientation API)
- Hover data streams: they diverge around cursor
- Fast scroll: holographic overlays flicker and distort

**Sound Design:**
- Synthwave: arpeggiated synth, 120bpm

**Sound Design:**

## STEP 4: ADVANCED EXPERIENCE DETAILS

### Scroll Speed Dynamics
| Speed | Effect |
|---|---|
| Slow | Photos develop, fabric unfurls, data streams build |
| Normal | Full choreographed experience |
| Fast | Flash cuts — archival aesthetic feels intentional |
| Very fast | Beat-driven flash transitions — becomes a music video |

### Slow Motion Moments
- Raised fist (Scene 2): 0.2x — honor the moment
- Creeper descent (Scene 3): 0.4x — fashion demands patience
- Leaping cat (Scene 7): 0.5x — the brand's signature in motion

### Dramatic Pauses
- Scene 3 at 35%: 1.5s scroll freeze — fabric billows
- Scene 6 at blank frame: 2s complete pause — user processes the revelation

### Key Wow Moments
1. **Scene 2 raise fist reveal** — emotional, unexpected
2. **Scene 3 kintsugi marble crack** — technical showcase
3. **Scene 4 exploded shoe** — immediately impressive to design clients
4. **Scene 6 gallery reveal** — meta-moment (user realizes they've been watching a brand story)

## STEP 5: TECHNICAL TRANSLATION

### Stack
```
Framework:    React Three Fiber
Animation:    GSAP ScrollTrigger
Post-FX:      Bloom, FilmGrain, HueSaturation, Vignette
Physics:      Custom Verlet cloth (Scene 3)
```

### Key Techniques

**Developing Photo Shader (Scene 1):**
```glsl
// Dissolve noise from sepia to full color based on scroll
float noise = snoise(uv * 8.0 + time * 0.1);
float reveal = smoothstep(0.0, 1.0, uProgress - noise * 0.3);
gl_FragColor = mix(sepia, texture, reveal);
```

**Kintsugi Crack Shader (Scene 3):**
- Voronoi fracture pre-computed offline
- Emissive channels activated along crack edges on trigger
- Gold material: metalness 1.0, roughness 0.1

**Exploded View (Scene 4):**
```javascript
// Each component moves from assembled position to exploded position
mesh.position.lerpVectors(assembledPos, explodedPos, scrollProgress);
```

**Holographic Data Shader (Scene 5):**
```glsl
float scanLine = step(0.98, sin(uv.y * 100.0 + time));
float alpha = 0.6 + scanLine * 0.4;
gl_FragColor = vec4(color, alpha * uProgress);
```
